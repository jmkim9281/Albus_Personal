LG Electronics makes home appliances, TVs and IT products sold worldwide, with KRW 89 trillion (about USD 60B+) in 2025 revenue. Concentrix, a global customer-experience company with about 455,000 employees, runs LG’s global website management project; LG is one of its largest B2B clients.

The situation and the challenge

Latin America is the second-highest-revenue region in LG's overseas sales organization. The region runs about 12 hours behind Seoul, so questions from local teams often waited until the next day, and it was hard to see where work was being held up. Within LATAM and MEA, six countries run more than one language on the same site, including two right-to-left languages.

Who was involved

Local digital managers in 21 countriesLG headquartersLG development teamDelivery centers in India and ChinaProduct information platform team

What I did

  1. May 2026Took over LATAM and MEA as Regional Manager
  2. HandoverWorked Latin American hours to see where requests stalled
  3. WeeklyAutomated launch readiness report for every subsidiary
  4. RolloutMoved launch confirmation from Excel to LG's platform
  • Expanded my working hours to overlap with Latin America for the handover period to see the bottleneck first-hand, then proposed a shift adjustment and additional staffing to close the time-zone gap.
  • Created and automated a weekly launch readiness report for the subsidiaries. Each report included all upcoming products as well as which countries have confirmed their product page data and which have not.
  • Moved all product launch confirmation information from Excel to LG's product information platform across LATAM and MEA: announcements, reminders and per-country support.

Key decisions

  • Work local hours myself before asking for more people.
    I needed to see where requests actually stalled before proposing a fix. The staffing proposal then rested on what I had seen, not on an assumption.
  • Report product launch readiness weekly by country.
    Local teams see their own gaps before launch day instead of after it, and headquarters sees which countries need a push.

In progress. Readiness figures are being collected on a consistent basis and will be added once there is enough history to compare.

What I took from it

Taking over a region is mostly about seeing where work waits. Once I could see that, most fixes were about clarifying who owns each step, not about working harder.
